Alessia Russo's Hat-Trick Sends Arsenal to Second in Women's Champions League

Glob News Desk · AI-assisted, human-reviewed · 2026-10-29 11:30 · 2 min read

Arsenal striker Alessia Russo scored a historic hat-trick against Benfica, becoming the highest English goalscorer in Women’s Champions League history.

Alessia Russo etched her name into the history books once again on Wednesday night, scoring a hat-trick to become the highest English goalscorer in the history of the UEFA Women’s Champions League. Her three-goal performance powered Arsenal to a crucial 3-0 victory over Benfica at Meadow Park, lifting the Gunners up to second place in the league phase table and providing a vital boost to their European campaign.

The 27-year-old forward has been in exceptional form recently, shaking off early-season struggles to find a rich vein of scoring touch. With ten goals in her past seven appearances for both club and country, Russo’s brilliance came at precisely the right moment for Arsenal, whose season had been marked by inconsistent results and unforced errors in the opening months.

Manager Renée Slegers praised Russo’s leadership and consistency, noting that the team has found a positive dynamic in recent matches. Slegers highlighted Russo’s role as a central figure who connects well with teammates and leads by example. The manager expressed satisfaction with the team’s intensity and connection during the match, stating that the players finally felt like themselves on the pitch.

Entering the contest, Arsenal faced significant pressure. Their European form had mirrored domestic difficulties, with a last-ditch win against HB Køge and a heartbreaking draw against Paris FC leaving them fifth in the standings before kickoff. The need for a statement result was clear, especially with top teams like Barcelona and OL Lyonnes looming in the standings.

Slegers made three changes to inject fresh energy into the squad, including returning Ona Batlle to her natural right-back position and giving full debuts to summer signings Élisa De Almeida and Géraldine Reuteler. Despite these adjustments, the first half was characterized by Arsenal’s dominance but an inability to break down a well-structured Benfica defense that arrived with confidence from consecutive domestic wins.

Benfica nearly took the lead early on when Frøya Dorsin missed a golden opportunity to lob a stranded goalkeeper, a moment that seemed to jolt Arsenal into higher gear. As the hosts increased their tempo, Olivia Smith created chances for both herself and Russo, forcing Benfica goalkeeper Lena Pauels into fine saves just before halftime.

Arsenal finally broke the deadlock shortly before the interval through a sublime finish from Russo following a well-worked move down the left flank. The goal acted as a switch, flipping the momentum entirely in favor of the home side, who began zipping the ball around with increasing ease and confidence in the second half.

Russo continued to orchestrate play, pressing and stretching the opposition until she doubled the lead by heading home a corner. She completed her hat-trick shortly after, converting a penalty after Caitlin Foord was fouled by Catarina Amado. This third goal marked the 200th milestone of Slegers’ tenure, easing pressure on the head coach and validating her tactical approach.

While sterner tests await, including an away trip to Barcelona in two weeks, this victory signaled more than just a rise in the rankings. It represented a return to form for a team that had struggled to express itself earlier in the season. With Russo leading the charge and the squad playing with renewed intensity, Arsenal appears ready to challenge for the top spots in Europe.
