Google Unveils Gemini 4 Carbon with Sharpened Cybersecurity Focus

Google releases its latest AI model, Gemini 4 Carbon, claiming top benchmark performance in coding and cybersecurity defense.

Google has officially launched Gemini 4 Carbon, its newest frontier artificial intelligence model, marking a significant step forward in the company’s ongoing race to dominate the generative AI landscape. Announced on Wednesday, the new model promises substantial improvements in software coding, autonomous task execution, and cybersecurity capabilities, positioning it as a formidable competitor to industry leaders like OpenAI and Anthropic.

In its announcement, Google highlighted that Gemini 4 Carbon currently holds the number one spot on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (v5.0). This benchmark ranks the model ahead of rival offerings such as Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. The release underscores Google’s assertion that it is accelerating the pace at which it pushes the boundaries of AI technology, particularly in areas requiring high precision and security awareness.

A primary focus of the new model is enhanced cyberdefense. Google states that Gemini 4 Carbon offers a significant leap in identifying vulnerabilities and generating secure code. It currently leads on SEC-bench Pro, a benchmark designed to measure an AI’s ability to discover real-world bugs, and ExploitGym, which evaluates how effectively a model can turn identified vulnerabilities into exploits. These metrics suggest the model is being optimized not just for creation, but for rigorous security testing and defense.

The model is also engineered for endurance and complexity. According to Google, internal teams have observed the system running for days to persist through dead ends while tackling complex, multi-step engineering tasks. This capability addresses a common limitation in earlier models, allowing Gemini 4 Carbon to handle long-horizon projects without losing context or efficiency. It is also reportedly better at avoiding shortcuts and resisting prompt injection attacks, where users attempt to manipulate the AI into bypassing safety protocols.

Transparency and honesty are additional pillars of the new release. Google claims that compared to its previous model, Argon, Gemini 4 Carbon cuts unsupported claims by half. This improvement aims to reduce hallucinations and provide more reliable outputs for professional use cases. The model is designed to collaborate more effectively with users, offering clearer reasoning and fewer speculative statements when dealing with uncertain data.

Initially, access to Gemini 4 Carbon is restricted to a select group of cybersecurity defense companies. These early partners will utilize the model through Google’s Fairwind Program, a framework designed to test and refine frontier models in critical infrastructure and security sectors. The company indicated that availability will eventually expand to other trusted partners as the technology matures and safety protocols are further validated.

Despite its advanced capabilities, the pricing structure remains competitive with its predecessor. Gemini 4 Carbon launches at the same introductory rate as Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens. This pricing strategy suggests Google’s intent to make powerful, specialized AI tools accessible to enterprise clients without imposing a steep premium for the latest generation of features.

The release of Gemini 4 Carbon comes amid intensifying competition in the AI sector, with major tech firms racing to demonstrate superiority in both raw intelligence and practical application. By emphasizing cybersecurity and long-term task persistence, Google is targeting specific enterprise needs that require reliability and security over mere speed or creativity. This strategic pivot may influence how businesses integrate AI into their development and security workflows.
