Brewers on Verge of History After Crushing Rays in World Series Game 3

Milwaukee routs Tampa Bay 10-1 to take a commanding 3-0 lead, needing just one win for their first-ever championship.

The Milwaukee Brewers are standing on the precipice of baseball history. On Monday night at Tropicana Field in St. Petersburg, Florida, the Brewers routed the Tampa Bay Rays 10-1 in Game 3 of the World Series, securing a commanding 3-0 series lead. With victory in Tuesday’s Game 4, Milwaukee will capture its first World Series title in franchise history, ending a championship drought that stretches back to 1982 and defying the odds of being down three games to none.

Rookie left-handed pitcher Robert Gasser set the tone early, delivering a masterclass in efficiency. Gasser allowed a line-drive single to Yandy Díaz leading off the bottom of the first and then retired 14 straight before Victor Mesa Jr. walked to start the sixth. With the help of a caught stealing and a double play, Gasser became the first starting pitcher to face the minimum 18 batters through six innings of a World Series game since Curt Schilling achieved the feat in 2001, and the first left-hander to do so since Sandy Koufax in 1965. His dominance helped Milwaukee build an insurmountable lead by the middle of the game.

The offensive explosion came in the fifth inning, where the Brewers recorded a postseason-record seven consecutive hits with two outs. The rally was sparked by Jackson Chourio, who went 4-for-5 at the plate, and Brice Turang, whose liner slipped past shortstop Taylor Walls to score two runs. Christian Yelich drew a walk, and William Contreras delivered a crucial infield single that loaded the bases after a replay overturn saved what would have been a force out. The inning ultimately produced eight runs, putting the game completely out of reach for Tampa Bay.

For the Rays, the loss marked a complete unraveling after a strong regular season where they led the American League with 98 wins. Starting pitcher Freddy Peralta, the MVP of the ALCS, struggled significantly against his former team. Peralta surrendered five runs and seven hits in just four and two-thirds innings before being pulled from the game. Manager Pat Murphy noted that while the Rays are a capable team, Milwaukee simply made every pitch count and played hard without giving in.

Historically, the situation looks bleak for Tampa Bay. Of the 43 previous major league teams to fall behind 3-0 in a best-of-seven postseason series, only the 2004 Boston Red Sox managed to rally and win four straight games. In the World Series specifically, of the 25 teams that have taken a 3-0 lead, 21 have gone on to sweep the series, and four have won in five games. The Rays, who have never won a World Series in their history, now face an uphill battle that has proven nearly impossible to overcome.

Despite the deficit, Rays third baseman Junior Caminero remained defiant after the loss. He assured fans that the team wants to give their best effort, acknowledging that Milwaukee is simply playing better and that things are going their way. The sellout crowd of 40,388 at Tropicana Field, many wearing white Devil Rays uniforms, cheered loudly from behind the visitors’ dugout, hoping to lift their team in front of their home fans.

Milwaukee’s path to this moment has been paved with resilience. Led by 67-year-old manager Pat Murphy, the Brewers entered the playoffs as the top seed in the National League with 103 wins. They swept the Los Angeles Dodgers in the NLCS after beating the San Diego Padres in the division series. This victory marks their eighth straight postseason win and fourth consecutive road victory, showcasing their ability to perform under pressure away from Miller Park.

The franchise’s history adds weight to this potential triumph. Originally known as the Seattle Pilots for a single season in 1969, the team moved to Milwaukee and became the Brewers in 1970. Their previous World Series appearance ended in a heartbreaking seven-game loss to the St. Louis Cardinals in 1982. A win on Tuesday would make them the first team to sweep both the League Championship Series and the World Series since the Cincinnati Reds accomplished the feat in 1976.

As the series shifts back to Milwaukee for Game 5 if necessary, all eyes will be on whether Tampa Bay can mount a historic comeback or if the Brewers will close out the series and etch their names into baseball lore. For now, Milwaukee holds the driver’s seat, needing just one more victory to fulfill decades of anticipation for their fanbase.
