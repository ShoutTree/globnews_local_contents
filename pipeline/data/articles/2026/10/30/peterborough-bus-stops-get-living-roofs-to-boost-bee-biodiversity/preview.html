Peterborough City Council approves plans for 34 bus stops to feature mini-gardens on their roofs, creating vital habitats for urban pollinators.

Peterborough City Council has officially approved a pioneering initiative to transform ordinary bus shelters into thriving micro-habitats for bees and other pollinators. The plan involves installing 'living roofs'—mini-gardens planted with wildflowers and vegetation—on top of 34 bus stops across the city. This move aims to boost local biodiversity by turning essential urban infrastructure into green spaces that support insect life.

The decision was made during a cabinet meeting on Tuesday, where council members discussed and endorsed the new strategy for managing the city's public transport infrastructure. The project is part of a broader overhaul of bus stop facilities, designed to enhance both environmental sustainability and passenger experience. By integrating nature into daily commute routes, the council hopes to raise awareness about the importance of pollinators in urban ecosystems.

Under the agreement, Bauer Media Outdoor UK will take over the management of Peterborough’s bus stops through a new contract. In exchange for managing the structures, the company will provide the council with an annual income of £140,000 from advertising sales, plus 2.5% of its quarterly gross revenue. The deal is expected to run for a minimum of ten years, with options to extend, potentially generating £2.8 million for the authority over two decades.

Chris Wiggin, the Liberal Democrat cabinet member responsible for the environment, strongly supported the initiative. He emphasized the critical role pollinators play in sustaining life, stating that without bees and other pollinators, there would be no other life. Wiggin expressed his satisfaction at seeing these plans come forward, noting that the council needs to do everything possible to support biodiversity and protect pollinator populations in the face of ongoing environmental challenges.

The introduction of living roofs is not unique to Peterborough but remains a relatively novel approach in many parts of the UK. There are currently more than 350 living roofs across the country, including installations in major cities such as Birmingham, Glasgow, and Northampton. These green structures help reduce heat island effects, manage stormwater runoff, and provide crucial nesting sites for insects, making them a valuable addition to urban planning strategies.

In addition to the ecological benefits, the new bus stop contracts include several upgrades aimed at improving passenger comfort and information access. Existing perch-style seats will be replaced with more comfortable seating arrangements. Furthermore, most bus stops will be equipped with digital advertising screens and up-to-date passenger information displays, ensuring travelers have real-time data on bus schedules and routes.
