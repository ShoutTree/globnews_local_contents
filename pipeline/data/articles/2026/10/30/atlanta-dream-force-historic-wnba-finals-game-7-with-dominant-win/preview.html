Atlanta Dream Force Historic WNBA Finals Game 7 with Dominant Win

The Atlanta Dream defeated the Golden State Valkyries 80-69 to level the series at 3-3, setting up the first-ever decisive seventh game in WNBA Finals history.

The Atlanta Dream refused to let a midday tipoff become their curtain call, overwhelming the Golden State Valkyries 80-69 on Thursday afternoon to force a decisive seventh game in the WNBA finals. Facing elimination at a lunchtime start, the Dream rediscovered their offensive touch just as their title hopes were threatening to slip away, sending the series back to San Francisco’s Chase Center for Saturday night’s winner-take-all finale.

Both franchises are seeking their first championship, and the extended best-of-seven format has finally delivered the ultimate decider after six games left nothing to separate the two teams. The Dream buried six three-pointers and shot an impressive 73.3% from the floor in a blistering opening quarter at State Farm Arena. They built a lead that ballooned to 25 points before halftime, effectively neutralizing the Valkyries' chances early in the contest.

Allisha Gray led the charge for Atlanta with a team-high 23 points, including five three-pointers. Rhyne Howard added 21 points, while Angel Reese notched her fourth double-double of the series with 15 points and 14 rebounds, along with six assists. Gray’s performance was particularly notable given she had been compromised by an injury to her shooting hand for most of the postseason, yet she answered crucial moments with precision when it mattered most.

For the Valkyries, Gabby Williams finished with a game-high 26 points after being held to just four in the first half. Tiffany Hayes chipped in 15 points, but Golden State struggled significantly from beyond the arc, shooting just 25.9% from three-point range compared to Atlanta’s efficiency. Veronica Burton endured a particularly frigid afternoon, making only two of her 13 shots from the floor and missing all six of her three-point attempts.

The victory came after the Dream had managed only 62 and 55 points in back-to-back defeats that erased their initial 2-1 series lead. On Thursday, they scored 32 points in the first quarter alone, making 11 of their first 15 shots and moving the ball with a fluidity that the Valkyries had largely disrupted over the previous two contests. The turnaround highlighted a resilience that had been tested throughout the playoffs.

Angel Reese attributed part of the team’s renewed confidence to a players-only meeting held before a film session. Forward Naz Hillmon urged her teammates to remember who they were, reminding them that everyone had been a superstar on their respective teams at some point. "We needed the best out of every single person tonight," Reese said, emphasizing the collective effort required to overcome the slump.

Although the Dream were never seriously threatened even when the Valkyries found their footing after the intermission, the game remained competitive until the final minutes. Golden State trimmed the deficit to 13 points early in the fourth quarter, only for Gray to hit a three-pointer followed by another from Reese to restore a 74-55 advantage with just under six minutes remaining. The Valkyries made one final push, getting as close as 77-69 on Williams’s three-point play with 1 minute and 15 seconds left, which fouled out Howard.

Gray sealed the victory with her fifth three-pointer off a Reese assist with 56 seconds remaining, removing all doubt. Golden State opted not to extend the contest by fouling in the closing seconds, leaving a final margin that barely hinted at the scale of the hosts’ dominance. The unusual 12:30 p.m. tipoff, a scheduling compromise that placed the biggest home game of Atlanta’s season in the middle of a workday, drew scrutiny but did not deter the sellout crowd of 17,099 fans.

Rhyne Howard expressed pride in the team's ability to maximize their opportunity despite the logistical challenges. "I knew that coming in, we had to be aggressive from the jump," Howard said. "It’s do or die. It’s win or go home." For Golden State coach Natalie Nakase, the loss was disappointing but not excusable due to the time zone difference. She noted that the team knew Atlanta would play desperately and failed to match that intensity, setting the stage for a historic showdown on Halloween night.
