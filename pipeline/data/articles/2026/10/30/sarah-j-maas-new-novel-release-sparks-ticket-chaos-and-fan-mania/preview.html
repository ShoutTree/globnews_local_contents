The launch of Sarah J Maas's latest romantasy book caused a UK bookstore's masquerade ball tickets to sell out in 13 minutes, highlighting the genre's massive cultural impact.

A new novel by bestselling author Sarah J Maas has triggered intense fan enthusiasm across the United Kingdom, demonstrating the commercial power of the romantasy genre. The release of A Court of Splintered Harmony, the first installment in the A Court of Thorns and Roses series in over five years, led to immediate logistical challenges for independent bookshops celebrating the event.

The Spicy Librarian, a romance-focused bookstore in Leamington Spa, announced plans for a masquerade ball to mark the occasion. The response was overwhelming: approximately 6,000 people attempted to purchase just 220 tickets. According to co-founder Ali Maynard James, the website crashed immediately upon launch, and all tickets were sold out within 13 minutes.

To accommodate the demand, the shop relocated the celebration to Ragley Hall, a stately home in Warwickshire. The event featured riders dressed as fae warriors carrying torches on horseback. Tickets were priced at £149. Due to the high interest, the shop held a second identical event the following night, both of which sold out completely.

The book went on sale at 12:01 am on Tuesday. It is the sixth volume in the series known to fans as Acotar, which began in 2015 with a dark retelling of Beauty and the Beast. By 2021, the series had sold 13 million copies. Across her three interlinked fantasy series, Maas has now sold more than 75 million books in 40 languages.

Maynard James noted that many attendees began reading the book immediately upon receiving it at midnight, with some finishing the entire volume within a few hours. The shop ordered approximately 750 copies to cover both balls and pre-orders, indicating strong initial stock requirements.

Romantasy, a genre blending fantasy and romance, has evolved from an online niche into one of publishing’s most lucrative categories. Last year, Onyx Storm by Rebecca Yarros recorded the biggest first week for a hardback novel in the UK since Harper Lee’s Go Set a Watchman in 2015. In 2024, robust sales in romance and fantasy helped push UK fiction revenue above £1 billion for the first time.

Starlin Marot, owner of Bad Girl Books in Oxford, the UK’s first romantasy-only shop, observed similar trends. Although she planned to open for only half an hour after midnight for a few readers who requested it on Instagram, a large queue formed outside. She described the scene as a crazy flurry of people, noting that the series often serves as a gateway drug into reading for young women.

In London, Main Character Books opted for a different approach, serving breakfast to readers collecting pre-orders and hosting an evening party for 45 guests. Owner Cassie Simmons reported that pre-orders in early October exceeded expectations, forcing them to double their order with the publisher. Sales have continued to move through stock faster than anticipated.

A Court of Splintered Harmony is the first part of a four-part story. The next two parts will be released together in A Court of Forgotten Melody on January 12, while the final installment is still being written.
