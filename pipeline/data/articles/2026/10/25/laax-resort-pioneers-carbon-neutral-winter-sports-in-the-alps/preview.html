Swiss ski resort Laax aims for carbon neutrality by 2030 through innovative energy-saving technologies and sustainable practices.

The Swiss alpine resort of Laax is implementing a comprehensive strategy to become carbon neutral by 2030, positioning itself as a leader in sustainable winter tourism. Facing the broader industry challenge of climate change impacts on snow reliability, this community near the Austrian border is aggressively cutting oil use, transitioning to sustainable energy generation, and aiming for zero food waste.

The initiative, described by CEO Reto Gurtner as 'greenstyle,' represents a significant shift from traditional ski resort operations. Gurtner, whose father built the first ski lifts in 1962, brings a background in economics and a passion for freestyle sports to the role. His vision integrates modern efficiency with the resort's history of embracing new sports like skateboarding and snowboarding since the 1970s.

A cornerstone of this transformation is the new gondola system in the neighboring village of Flims. Utilizing a complex algorithm, the system operates on an on-demand basis, dispatching individual cabins to one of five mountain destinations based on real-time skier choices. This approach eliminates the energy waste associated with empty gondolas traveling up and down the mountainside, balancing passenger demand with operational efficiency.

Beyond transportation, the resort is addressing its environmental footprint through technological upgrades in snow management. Laax has introduced chemical-free snow cannons and electric piste-grooming vehicles, reducing reliance on fossil fuels for maintaining slopes. These measures are critical as lower-altitude resorts across Europe face increasing threats from slushy conditions and unpredictable weather patterns.

Despite these efforts, the resort maintains that it is not entirely dependent on natural snowfall. The halfpipe and freestyle playgrounds require minimal snow cover and are maintained using hydroelectric power. Additionally, the resort offers extensive summer activities, including mountain biking and hiking, which provide revenue streams independent of winter conditions.

Local guides note that the cultural landscape of the resort is also evolving. With visitors coming from diverse global backgrounds, including recent groups from the Middle East, the resort is adapting to a more international clientele. This demographic shift coincides with the physical changes brought on by climate adaptation strategies.

As other regions struggle with the economic implications of dwindling snow seasons, Laax’s model offers a potential blueprint for the future of winter sports. By prioritizing energy efficiency and diversifying its offerings, the resort aims to remain viable and environmentally responsible in a changing climate.
